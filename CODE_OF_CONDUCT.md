# Code of Conduct

This project maps crimes against women in India. Behind every number is a real person, so we ask
everyone who takes part (in issues, pull requests, discussions or anywhere else the project is
represented) to treat the subject, the data and each other with care.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make taking part in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless
of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, caste, religion, region,
language, nationality, personal appearance, race, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and
healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that helps:

- Being respectful of differing viewpoints, experiences and interpretations of the data
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Citing sources for every figure, and correcting mistakes openly when they are found
- Keeping discussion focused on patterns in the data, not on individuals
- Remembering that survivors may be reading, and writing accordingly

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- Sexualised language or imagery, or sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Victim-blaming, or making light of violence against women or children
- Using the data to stereotype or target any community, caste, religion or region
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their
  explicit permission
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Protecting identities

This project works only with aggregate statistics. To keep it that way:

- **Never share anything that could identify a victim or survivor**: names, photos, addresses,
  family details, schools, workplaces or case specifics. Indian law prohibits disclosing the
  identity of survivors of sexual offences, and this applies here too.
- **Do not name individual accused persons**, including elected representatives. ADR data on
  declared cases is used only as aggregate counts.
- **Do not post links to videos, images or news reports that identify a victim.**
- If you find identifying information anywhere in the repository or on the site, report it (see
  below) rather than quoting it in a public issue.

Content that breaks these rules will be removed without discussion.

## Data integrity

- Add or change figures only with a public, citable source (NCRB, Census, ADR, or similar).
- Do not alter, round or omit data to support a particular narrative.
- If the data is missing for a year, state or category, the dashboard should say so rather than
  fill the gap with estimates presented as fact.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and will take
appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive or harmful.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that
do not align with this Code of Conduct, and will explain moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces, and also applies when an individual is
officially representing the project in public spaces.

## Reporting

- **General concerns** (for example a disrespectful comment or a data error): open an issue on
  GitHub.
- **Sensitive reports** (identifying information, harassment, or anything you should not post
  publicly): use GitHub's **Report content** option on the comment, issue or repository, or open an
  issue titled "Private report requested" without any details, and a maintainer will arrange a
  private channel.

All reports will be reviewed promptly and fairly, and the privacy of the reporter will be respected.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will follow these guidelines in deciding the consequences of a breach:

1. **Correction.** For inappropriate language or other minor unprofessional behaviour: a private
   written warning explaining the problem, and possibly a request for a public apology.
2. **Warning.** For a single serious incident or repeated minor ones: a warning with consequences
   for continued behaviour, and no interaction with the people involved for a set period.
3. **Temporary ban.** For a serious breach, including sustained inappropriate behaviour: a
   temporary ban from any interaction with the project.
4. **Permanent ban.** For a pattern of breaches, harassment of an individual, aggression towards or
   disparagement of any group, or disclosing a survivor's identity: a permanent ban from the
   project.
